# Competitive Intelligence & Strategic Bet · Roamly Groups

## 1. Primary competitor

- **Competitor:** Airbnb Experiences
- **Core offering:** Locally hosted activities, including private group experiences, with guest invitations, shared reservation details, and group messaging
- **How they position themselves:** Explore a city through locals who know it best, with accommodation, experiences, and services available in one app. This directly overlaps with Roamly’s promise of authentic local experiences
- **Primary audience:** Travelers seeking locally hosted experiences, including friends and families booking activities together. Private bookings also accommodate work outings and other gatherings
- **Known pricing or packaging:** Pricing varies by experience. Customers can book available spots or reserve a private group where the host offers it. Private groups have a host-set minimum price; if the group’s total exceeds that minimum, the higher amount applies
- **Why they're the biggest threat:** Airbnb competes for the same customer, occasion, and purchase: friends booking an authentic local experience together. It already combines relevant inventory with group-booking and communication features, making it a close substitute—not simply a recognizable brand

### Competitive Battlecard: Roamly Groups vs. Airbnb Experiences

#### 1. Know the Landscape

##### Competitor Overview
Airbnb Experiences competes for the same purchase: friends booking locally hosted activities together. Its authenticity positioning directly overlaps with Roamly’s.

##### Key Offerings
- Locally hosted experiences and private group bookings where available.
- Guest invitations, shared reservation details, and group messaging.
- Accommodation, services, and experiences in one app with a trip itinerary. [1][2]

##### Pricing
- Prices vary by experience.
- Private groups have a host-set minimum; the higher group total applies when it exceeds that minimum.
- No verified evidence that Roamly is cheaper. [1]

#### 2. Understand the Battlefield

##### Key Strengths
- **Positioning overlap:** Local hosts and authentic experiences are established parts of its offer.
- **Trip integration:** Accommodation and activities can stay within one app.
- **Group functionality:** Private bookings, invitations, and messaging make it a credible substitute. [1][2]

##### Key Weaknesses / Openings
- **Pre-booking coordination:** Documented group invitations follow confirmation. This is a potential opening, not proof that Airbnb lacks other pre-booking tools. [1]
- **Private availability:** Depends on the host. Roamly must validate its own availability before claiming superiority. [1]
- **Payment uncertainty:** Help documentation restricts multiple payment methods for payment in full, while published terms mention an eligible group-payment service. Verify market and checkout eligibility before claiming exclusive split payments. [3][4]

#### 3. Win the Deal

##### Why You Win
**Target winning situation:** An existing Roamly customer wants to bring friends, but separate bookings and payment collection block the purchase.

Roamly Groups connects coordinated booking, split payments, and shared itineraries within a platform the organizer already uses.

**Defensibility:** Features are copyable. The stronger potential defense combines existing customer relationships, vetted host supply, and reliable group-booking execution. Neither exclusive inventory nor superior group conversion has been established.

##### Why They Win
- The group already organizes its trip in Airbnb.
- Airbnb offers a better-fitting experience, price, or available time.
- One person is comfortable paying, making split payments less valuable.
- Roamly introduces delays or confusion around unpaid participants, cancellations, or refunds.

These are likely losing situations to validate, not observed win/loss results.

##### Why You
**Against the status quo:** Book with friends without managing separate reservations or collecting everyone’s money manually.

The outcome must be less organizer work and a confirmed experience—not another planning tool.

#### 4. Handle the Hard Moments

##### Quick Dismiss
“Airbnb already offers private experiences and group messaging. Roamly Groups focuses on coordinating the booking and splitting payment within the experience platform you already use. The deciding question is whether that makes your group easier to get booked.”

##### Landmine Questions
Use these to evaluate both products, not to imply unverified competitor gaps:

- Can each friend pay their share toward the same reservation?
- What happens to availability when one participant has not paid?
- Can everyone review the plan before confirmation?
- How are payments and refunds handled if someone drops out?
- How much chasing does the organizer still do outside the platform?

##### Objection Handling

**“Airbnb already does group bookings.”**  
Yes. Our case depends on easier coordination and payment, not unique access to group booking. Compare the full journey through confirmation.

**“We already use Airbnb for the trip.”**  
That is a real convenience advantage. Roamly must offer a preferred experience or meaningfully reduce organizer effort to earn the booking.

**“We can use a group chat and reimburse one person.”**  
If that works smoothly, there may be little reason to change. Roamly is most relevant when separate bookings and payment collection create friction.

**“Airbnb is cheaper.”**  
Compare total price, group size, inclusions, and cancellation terms. We have no established price advantage; if equivalent options are cheaper there, we may lose.

**“What makes this hard to copy?”**  
Split payments and shared itineraries are not a moat. Trusted relationships, dependable host supply, and proven execution are potential defenses that we must demonstrate.

#### GTM Decision

**Keep PLG + Expand. Lead with reduced organizer effort.**

Validate fully paid, confirmed bookings and support burden against the existing workflow before claiming superiority. Test Airbnb’s relevant checkout before publishing payment comparisons.

## 3. What I want to revisit in my Module 1 strategy

I want to revisit **my value proposition** because Airbnb already offers local experiences, group bookings, and shared trip information. Our feature list alone does not establish a compelling reason to choose Roamly. I would sharpen the promise around **getting friends committed, paid, and confirmed with less organizer effort**, then test whether we actually deliver that outcome better than the alternatives.

## 4. Strategic bet

### Data
- **Who Roamly Groups is for:** I’m doubling down on **existing Roamly customers organizing local experiences for friends traveling together**, prioritizing those struggling with separate bookings, coordination, and collecting payments.

The scenario shows customers attempting group bookings, using manual workarounds, and abandoning the process. The battlecard shows that Airbnb already offers local experiences and group-booking features, so our opportunity must center on reducing the organizer’s effort—not simply offering group bookings.

The competitive research has not revealed evidence that another segment is more attractive. It sharpens the problem we need to solve for our original audience. We will include groups with fixed plans and those still coordinating, then test how planning readiness affects completion.

**Our commitment:** Launch for the existing customer who takes responsibility for organizing an experience with friends. This remains a strategic choice to validate; the available data does not yet prove that friend groups are the largest or most profitable segment.
- **The one thing worth choosing it for:** Less work for the organizer to get friends committed, paid, and confirmed for one local experience. Coordinated booking and split payments support that outcome. This is the advantage we intend to prove, not an established superiority claim.
- **The biggest threat to that position:** Airbnb could deliver an equally easy coordination and payment flow within the app the group already uses. Because these features are copyable, Roamly must demonstrate a meaningful reduction in organizer effort; feature availability alone will not sustain the position.

### Insight
The signal that changes my view is that **group booking already exists, yet coordination remains a problem for Roamly customers**. The opportunity is not simply letting people book together; it is reducing the work required to turn individual interest into a confirmed group experience.

Airbnb’s integrated trip experience gives customers a reason to stay where they already plan. To win that buyer, Roamly must make the organizer’s job meaningfully easier—from coordinating friends to completing payment—not just offer similar features.

That shifts our focus from promoting group-booking functionality to proving **less chasing and more fully paid, confirmed bookings**.

### Belief
I now believe that Roamly Groups wins when it helps existing customers get their friends committed, paid, and confirmed with less chasing, because reducing the organizer’s workload gives them a concrete reason to choose it over another booking platform or their usual group-chat workaround.

### Bet
- **I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by:** I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by making it easier for existing customers to turn plans with friends into fully paid, confirmed experiences through self-service coordination and split payments—prioritizing less organizer effort over broader trip-planning features or competing on price.
- **To make this bet, I am deprioritizing:** To make this bet, I am deprioritizing corporate groups, broad acquisition campaigns, and full-trip planning features so we can focus on helping existing customers coordinate and complete bookings with friends.
