# GTM Strategy · Roamly Groups

## 1. Discover Framework

### Align on the Goal
- **Core business problem:** Roamly is losing bookings from customers who already want to book group experiences because its product makes coordination difficult. Separate accounts, manual split payments, and fragmented planning create friction and high drop-off. The business opportunity is to convert this demonstrated demand into completed group bookings while preserving the experience that makes individual and small-party customers return.
- **What success looks like:** - Business: More completed group bookings and incremental revenue, rather than simply shifting existing bookings into a new format.
- Product: Less abandonment during group booking and fewer support requests related to coordination, without reducing individual and small-party booking performance.
- Brand: Roamly becomes a trusted choice for shared, authentic local experiences while maintaining its reputation for quality.
- Customer relationship: Organizers spend less time managing logistics, participants can coordinate and pay more easily, and successful experiences encourage repeat group bookings and referrals.

Success means making group demand a sustainable source of growth while protecting customer satisfaction and host experience.

### Do the Research
- **Signals of a real opportunity:** Customers are already trying to organize larger group bookings despite the product’s limitations. They coordinate across accounts, split payments manually, and work around availability and preference gaps. These behaviors show existing demand for group experiences.

High drop-off in these sessions, combined with consistent customer feedback, suggests that coordination friction is preventing bookings. We should validate how much abandonment comes from that friction versus price, availability, or other factors.

Roamly’s repeat booking rate—three times the industry average—and its established host network strengthen the opportunity. They indicate strong customer value and a foundation for serving groups, although neither proves demand in a specific segment.
- **Top 5 possible segments:** Segment - Primary need
1. Friend groups traveling together - Agree on an experience and book together without one person collecting everyone’s money.
2. Multigenerational families on vacation - Coordinate different ages, preferences, and availability in one shared itinerary.
3. Celebration groups - Organize a dependable experience for a birthday, bachelor/bachelorette trip, or reunion.
4. Corporate teams on retreats or offsites - Book quality team experiences with straightforward coordination and payment.
5. Travel clubs and community groups - Arrange experiences for members with shared interests and manage participation.
- **How to size the opportunity:** What we need to know - Where we would look
How many customers attempt group bookings, their party sizes, and where they abandon - Booking funnel data, session behavior, support tickets, and customer surveys
Which segments drive those attempts, how frequently they book, and their typical spending - Interviews with organizers and participants, booking history, and targeted surveys
How much abandonment Roamly Groups could recover - Pilot results and comparisons with the existing booking flow
Willingness to pay and expected revenue and margin per group booking - Customer research, pricing tests, and internal finance data
Whether hosts can accommodate demand by city, activity, group size, and season - Host capacity data, availability calendars, and host interviews
How much demand exists beyond current users and how reachable it is - Travel market reports, competitor offerings, search trends, and relevant communities


I would begin with the existing customer base: annual group-booking attempts × expected improvement in completion rate × average booking value provides an initial estimate of recoverable booking value. Applying Roamly’s revenue share would estimate platform revenue.

I would then size expansion into the most promising segment, accounting for acquisition costs, host capacity, seasonality, and bookings that would have happened anyway.

Analytics - Size segment demand, establish funnel baselines, and measure conversion, repeat bookings, and incremental growth.

### Form Your Hypothesis
- **Most valuable segment to target first, and why:** I would target friend groups traveling together, starting with existing Roamly customers organizing an experience for their friends.

This segment closely matches the coordination problems described in the scenario: aligning preferences and availability, booking across accounts, and collecting payments. It also builds on Roamly’s existing reputation for authentic experiences and its reach through travel communities and social sharing.

An existing customer organizing a group could introduce Roamly to new participants, creating a potential acquisition benefit alongside the booking itself. Compared with corporate teams, this segment is also less likely to require additional purchasing processes such as procurement approvals and corporate invoicing.

This is my initial bet, not a proven ranking. I would validate segment size, booking frequency, conversion potential, and profitability before expanding.
- **Core value proposition:** With Roamly Groups, friends can coordinate and book a curated local experience through one shared booking flow, with split payments and a shared itinerary, instead of piecing together separate bookings and collecting money manually.

## 2. GTM motion

- **Chosen motion:** PLG
- **Why:** The product directly addresses the barriers preventing existing customers from completing group bookings. Our first move is to help those customers discover and use Roamly Groups, then make participation easy for the friends they bring.

Travel communities and social sharing would support awareness and distribution, but they would not make this a community-led motion. The core conversion mechanism is the self-service product, and we have not established that an ongoing community is necessary for adoption.

Our key assumption is that groups can reach a confirmed booking without human assistance. We should validate this alongside whether invited participants later book or organize another experience.

## 4. The assumption I'm pushing back on

I’m pushing back on the recommendation to limit our target segment to groups that have already agreed on their destination and dates. The scenario does not establish that this is the most valuable audience, and requiring fixed dates could exclude customers who need Roamly Groups precisely to coordinate availability.

I agree that interest from one organizer does not guarantee commitment from the group. However, I would test planning readiness rather than make it an immediate targeting restriction. We should compare fully paid, confirmed booking rates for groups with fixed plans versus those still coordinating, then use the evidence to refine our segment.

## 5. V1 GTM strategy one-pager

**Business opportunity**  
Convert existing demand for group experiences into completed bookings by reducing coordination and payment friction, while protecting the individual and small-party experience.

**Target segment**  
Existing Roamly customers organizing local experiences for friends traveling together. Include both groups with fixed plans and those still coordinating. Treat planning readiness as a variable to test, not an eligibility requirement.

**Value proposition**  
Book an authentic local experience with your friends without juggling separate bookings or collecting everyone’s money—with coordinated group booking, split payments, and a shared itinerary.

**Primary goal: Expand**  
Generate incremental bookings and revenue from existing customers. Acquisition through invited friends is a secondary opportunity.

**GTM motion: Product-led growth**  
Enable organizers and participants to coordinate and book through self-service. The product should deliver value without sales assistance; participant invitations are a potential growth mechanism to validate.

**Recommended channel approach**

- **In-product discovery:** Test Roamly Groups prompts at relevant experience-browsing and booking moments.
- **Targeted email:** Introduce the feature to existing customers with identifiable group-booking intent, using organizer-focused messaging.
- **Participant invitations:** Make joining the group, viewing plans, and completing payment straightforward. Measure whether participants later become organizers.
- **Travel communities and social:** Use as supporting channels once the pilot demonstrates successful bookings and credible customer stories.

**Initial launch and validation**  
Pilot in a limited set of cities and experiences selected for observed demand and suitable host capacity. Compare outcomes for groups with fixed plans and those still coordinating, using the same definition of a successful booking. Investigate unpaid participants, abandonment, and support needs before expanding.

**Primary success metric**  
Incremental fully paid, confirmed group bookings per eligible existing customer during the pilot, compared with a comparable holdout using the current experience. Define eligibility before exposure and set a numerical target after establishing the baseline.

Track booking completion and time to confirmation as supporting measures. Monitor cancellations, support demand, host satisfaction, and total bookings per customer to detect disruption or substitution.

**Core hypothesis**  
If existing customers can coordinate and pay for experiences with friends more easily, more group demand will become completed bookings. Planning readiness may affect conversion; pilot evidence will determine whether we narrow the audience.
