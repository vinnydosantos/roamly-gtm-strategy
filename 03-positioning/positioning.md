# Positioning Framework & Statement · Roamly Groups

## 1. Positioning framework

- **For (target audience):** For existing Roamly customers who take responsibility for organizing a local experience for friends traveling together and find themselves chasing decisions, coordinating separate bookings, and collecting everyone’s money.
- **Who want / need:** I want to get an experience booked for all of us without constantly chasing friends for decisions and payments or having to pay for everyone upfront.
- **We offer:** We offer Roamly Groups, a self-service way for friends to coordinate and book a local experience hosted by a vetted expert, split the payment, and access a shared itinerary in one place.
- **Because of (value proposition):** Because it helps organizers turn plans with friends into a confirmed experience with less chasing and no need to collect everyone’s money themselves, so they can enjoy the trip instead of managing it.
- **Despite (barriers):** Despite requiring friends to adopt a shared booking flow instead of their familiar group chat, and still depending on each person to commit and pay—meaning the organizer may need to chase late participants.
- **Better than (differentiation):** Better than coordinating separate bookings in a group chat and collecting reimbursements manually, Roamly Groups connects group booking, split payments, and shared plans within the platform the organizer already uses. Against Airbnb Experiences, our intended advantage is less organizer effort from planning to confirmation—an advantage we must validate, not claim as proven.

## 2. Positioning statement

### First draft
Roamly Groups lets existing Roamly customers coordinate and book local experiences with friends, split payments, and share an itinerary in one place. It helps organizers get the group booked with less chasing and no manual money collection, so they can enjoy the experience too.

### Pressure test
- **Test I ran:** B · Competitor discomfort
- **What it revealed:** **As an Airbnb Experiences product leader: almost.** Replace “Roamly” with “Airbnb,” and most of this could describe our offer. The exception is “split payments” and “no manual money collection”—we would need to verify those capabilities for the relevant bookings before using that wording.

What makes it too generic:

- **“Local experiences with friends”** describes the category.
- **“In one place”** is a convenience claim that fits our integrated travel app.
- **“Existing Roamly customers”** identifies your launch audience but does not explain why your product is better.
- **“Less chasing”** is a compelling outcome, but the statement offers no evidence that Roamly delivers it better.

**Your strongest potential distinction is removing the organizer’s role as money collector.** That addresses a specific burden. However, payment functionality is copyable, and exclusivity has not been established.

My verdict: **clear and relevant, but not yet ownable.** Focus on the organizer’s payment burden, then substantiate the promise through the actual booking experience. Sharper wording alone cannot create a competitive advantage.

### Final statement
Roamly Groups is a shared booking and payment flow for existing Roamly customers who organize local experiences for their friends. It brings the group’s booking, split payments, and itinerary together so the organizer can get everyone booked without becoming the group’s money collector.
